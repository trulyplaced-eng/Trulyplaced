TrulyPlaced vs LazyApply

An honest comparison · Checked 12 November 2026

LazyApply and TrulyPlaced take opposite approaches. LazyApply is an AI agent that fills in applications at high volume, from 15 to 1,500 a day, on a yearly plan. TrulyPlaced is a career specialist who rewrites your resume and sends 25–30 tailored applications every business day, on a one-time plan.

The short answer. Choose LazyApply if you want the most applications for the least money and are comfortable with automated, untailored applications. Choose TrulyPlaced if you'd rather send fewer, tailored applications prepared by a person, with your resume rewritten first.

Volume or tailoring?

The real choice is between many applications and well-matched ones. Sending hundreds a day can work in some fields, but recruiters read the applications, and a generic one is easy to pass over. TrulyPlaced sends fewer, each matched to a role that fits you, with the wording of your resume adjusted to the job and your facts left exactly as they are.

Which should you choose?

  • Tight budget, high-volume roles: LazyApply.
  • You want a person preparing each application: TrulyPlaced.
  • Your resume needs work first: TrulyPlaced, where the rewrite is included.
  • You want automation but tailored to each job, at low cost: TrulyPlaced Autopilot, our self-serve app, free to start.

Questions people ask

Is LazyApply cheaper than TrulyPlaced?

Yes. LazyApply's yearly plans start at $99, while TrulyPlaced's one-time plans start at $299 for 250 applications. The difference is that a person rewrites your resume and prepares each TrulyPlaced application.

Does LazyApply tailor each application?

Its pricing page describes filling in applications from your information and up to 20 resume profiles; it does not state that each application is tailored to the job.

What's LazyApply's refund policy?

A 30-day money-back guarantee on the first purchase only, if you've applied to fewer than 100 jobs and the product didn't work as advertised, according to its refund policy page.
